Undeclared Origin

The label does not say. That silence is the finding.

Most ingredients are not hidden. They are simply never explained.

72%
In our first analysis of real supermarket products, 72% of results could not be confirmed — not because the ingredients were forbidden, but because the label never declared where they came from.

Based on BOTIA's own scans of supermarket products between March and April 2026, mostly in the Netherlands and Belgium, using the Muslim layer only. Repeated scans of the same product were counted once.

Why Mashbooh is the most important state

Classifying an ingredient as Mashbooh is not an accusation — it is a signal of uncertainty. Food labels are legal documents before they are human explanations. When the label omits the origin of an additive or the method of slaughter, BOTIA makes that gap visible so you can decide.

Scientific evidence works with what is declared. Personal choice needs to know where it came from. That is the difference — and that is where the label goes silent.

Why do these doubts arise?

Label Aspect General Regulation Halal Standard Why BOTIA detects ambiguity
Additives (E-numbers) & Enzymes Can be grouped by function without specifying vegetable or animal origin. Requires all additives to come strictly from lawful sources. If an additive could be animal-derived but the label is silent, BOTIA marks it as doubtful.
Meat from permitted animals Requires declaring the animal species. Requires lawful slaughter by a Muslim invoking Allah and cutting specific veins. Labels almost never detail the slaughter method, so BOTIA cannot assume compliance.
