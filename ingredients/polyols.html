Polyols

sugar alcohols

What it is

Polyols, also called sugar alcohols, are substances that sweeten and add bulk. They are commonly used in chewing gum, sweets, pastries and desserts where some or all of the sugar is replaced. Common examples include sorbitol, xylitol, maltitol, erythritol, mannitol, isomalt and lactitol. They are not sugars, but neither do they work like intense sweeteners such as aspartame: as well as sweetness, they add body and bulk. Absorption and metabolism vary between polyols. In sufficient amounts, some can cause gastrointestinal symptoms. In the European Union, foods containing more than 10% added authorised polyols must carry the warning that excessive consumption may produce laxative effects. They also contribute a different amount of energy from sugar. For EU energy calculation, polyols count as 2.4 kcal per gram and erythritol as 0 kcal per gram.

Why BOTIA flags it

BOTIA flags polyols because they often appear in products where sugar has been replaced, and because “sugar-free” does not tell you what was used instead. They also matter because EU rules explicitly require a laxative-effects warning above a defined amount of added polyols. The profile also connects the aggregate nutrition value with the specific polyols named in the ingredient list.

BOTIA CAN

  • Read the declared total and keep its unit and its basis.
  • Identify which specific polyols appear in the ingredient list and link to their record.
  • Explain the laxative-effects warning and when EU rules require it.
  • State that the energy value of polyols is calculated with a different factor from sugar, and which one applies in that market.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Say how much of each individual polyol there is. The label may declare the total; the ingredient list says which are present, but not necessarily in what amount.
  • Predict how they will affect you. Tolerance varies a great deal between people and between polyols.
  • Say that a product with polyols is better or worse than one with sugar. BOTIA shows what is in it; the decision is yours.
  • Treat an undeclared value as zero. It is an optional field in many labelling systems.
