Energy

What it is

The energy in a food is what it provides when metabolised. It can be expressed in kilojoules and kilocalories, and both units measure the same thing: one kilojoule is approximately 0.239 kilocalories. For nutrition labelling, the figure is calculated by applying conversion factors to the declared nutrients. In the European Union, for example, the factors are 4 kcal per gram of protein, 4 per gram of carbohydrate — except polyols —, 9 per gram of fat, 7 per gram of alcohol, 2 per gram of fibre and 2.4 per gram of polyols. Erythritol counts as 0. In the European Union energy is declared in kJ and kcal. China uses kJ as the regulatory unit. In the United States and Canada the nutrition panel shows Calories.

Why BOTIA flags it

BOTIA flags energy because it is the number most people look at and the one most easily misread. The reference that goes with it — 2,000 kcal, 8,400 kJ in the European Union — is a reference value for adults, fixed by convention so that labelling is possible. It is not what you need: that depends on your size, your age, your activity and your health. And because the figure comes from a calculation, not a measurement. Two products with the same energy can have very different compositions.

BOTIA CAN

  • Read both units where they are declared and keep the basis: per 100 g, per 100 ml, per serving or per package.
  • Show the reference that applies in the market where the product was labelled, and name the authority that publishes it.
  • Explain that the figure is calculated from the nutrients using fixed factors, and state which factors apply in that market.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Tell you what percentage of your own needs this represents. The reference value is a labelling convention for adult populations, not an individual requirement.
  • Say whether a product's energy is high or low. That depends on what the product is, how much is eaten, and what else is eaten that day.
  • Work out composition from energy. Two products with the same figure can split it very differently between fat, carbohydrate and protein.
  • Convert between units where the market does not require it without marking the conversion as derived.
