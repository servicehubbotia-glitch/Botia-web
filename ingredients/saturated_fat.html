Saturated fat

What it is

Saturated fats are fatty acids with no double bonds. Many fats rich in saturated fatty acids are solid or semi-solid at room temperature, such as butter or lard; they are also present in foods and oils such as meat, coconut and palm. On a nutrition label they form part of total fat and are also declared as a subfield in many markets, including the European Union, the United States and Canada. The figure groups different fatty acids. Stearic, palmitic and lauric acid all count as saturated fat, but they are not identical molecules, and research examines their effects separately as well as the food sources they come from. The label does not provide that breakdown. It declares the group.

Why BOTIA flags it

BOTIA flags saturated fat because the label compresses it into one figure, while scientific evidence also examines which fatty acid is involved, what replaces it in the diet and which food it comes from. WHO's 2023 guideline continues to recommend that saturated fat provide no more than 10% of total energy. At the same time, research on chain length, nutrient replacement and food matrix shows that a single number does not capture the whole context. BOTIA does not turn that debate into a verdict. It shows the declared amount, the applicable official reference and the sources that explain the limits of the figure.

BOTIA CAN

  • Read the declared value and keep its unit and its basis.
  • Show the reference that applies in the market where the product was labelled, naming the authority and the date.
  • Present the WHO target as population context, identified as such and not as a percentage of what this product contributes to you.
  • Identify the oils and fats in the ingredient list that provide them, where they can be recognised.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Tell you what percentage of your own needs this value represents. Reference intakes are set by each authority for adult populations.
  • Distinguish which specific saturated fatty acids the product contains. The label declares the group, not its composition.
  • Say whether they are good or bad. Official bodies maintain recommendations to limit them, and more recent research qualifies that they do not all behave the same. BOTIA shows both.
  • Apply one country's front-of-pack threshold to a product labelled in another.
  • Treat an undeclared value as zero.
