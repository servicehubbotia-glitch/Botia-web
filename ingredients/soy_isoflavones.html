Soy Isoflavones

soy isoflavones, soya isoflavones, soybean isoflavones, isoflavones, genistein, daidzein, glycitein, soy isoflavone extract, soya isoflavone extract

What it is

Naturally occurring soy compounds such as genistein, daidzein and glycitein. Their structures resemble oestrogen enough to interact with oestrogen receptors, but their biological behaviour is not the same as human oestrogen.

Why BOTIA flags it

Soy isoflavones resemble oestrogens enough to bind to their receptors, but they do not behave like human oestrogen. At amounts normally consumed as food, human studies have not shown the hormonal effects people commonly fear. Eating soy food is not the same as taking concentrated isoflavone extracts; even for supplements, EFSA found no evidence of harm to the breast, uterus or thyroid within the doses and durations it assessed in peri- and post-menopausal women. An ingredient name alone cannot predict an individual's hormonal response. Soy isoflavones do not do the same thing in everyone. Some people's gut bacteria turn one of them into S‑equol and some people's do not, and that is not something the label can tell you.

BOTIA CAN

  • Identify explicitly declared soy isoflavones, isoflavone extracts and named compounds such as genistein, daidzein and glycitein.
  • Distinguish an explicitly declared concentrated isoflavone ingredient from an ordinary soy ingredient whose isoflavone dose is not stated.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Calculate the isoflavone dose from an ordinary soy ingredient when the label provides no quantity.
  • Predict an individual's hormonal, menopausal, thyroid or other clinical response from the ingredient name alone.
