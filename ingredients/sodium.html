Salt

salt, salt equivalent

What it is

Salt is sodium chloride. In food it is not there only for taste: it preserves, because it makes it harder for bacteria to grow; it changes texture, because it alters how proteins behave in bread or cured meat; and it brings out other flavours, including sweetness. That is why it turns up in products where nobody expects it, such as pastries, breakfast cereals or biscuits. On a nutrition label, though, "salt" does not mean only the salt that was added. In the European Union and several other markets, the declared figure is the salt equivalent of all the sodium in the product, calculated as sodium × 2.5. That total includes sodium from any source: added salt, but also raising agents, flavour enhancers, preservatives and stabilisers that contain it. Other markets do not declare salt at all. They declare sodium, and the two figures are not interchangeable without stating that a conversion was applied.

Why BOTIA flags it

BOTIA flags salt because the same product can carry a very different figure depending on where it was labelled, and because the number does not mean what most people assume. A product with a high salt figure may not taste salty at all: sodium from bicarbonate, monosodium glutamate, sodium nitrite or sodium phosphates all count towards it. A product that tastes salty may declare less than expected. BOTIA shows the figure as the label declares it, states which reference that market uses, and says when a conversion has been applied.

BOTIA CAN

  • Read the declared salt value and keep its unit and its basis: per 100 g, per 100 ml, per serving or per package.
  • Show the reference that applies in the market where the product was labelled, and name the authority that publishes it.
  • Mark any conversion between sodium and salt as derived, showing the original declared field alongside it.
  • Explain that the declared figure covers sodium from every source in the product, not only added salt.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Tell you what percentage of your own needs this value represents. Reference intakes are set for adult populations by each authority; they are not individual requirements, and they differ by age, sex and circumstances.
  • Treat salt and sodium as the same declaration. They are separate fields and a product may declare one, the other, or both.
  • Convert between salt and sodium where the market does not define a conversion, or apply one without saying so.
  • Tell you how much of the sodium came from added salt and how much from other ingredients. The label declares the total; it does not break it down.
  • Treat an undeclared value as zero. If a market does not require salt to be declared and the label does not show it, BOTIA reports that it is not declared.
