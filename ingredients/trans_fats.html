Trans fatty acids

trans fats, trans fatty acids, partially hydrogenated oil, partially hydrogenated vegetable oil, iTFAs, grasas trans

What it is

Trans fats are a type of unsaturated fat. Industrial trans fats come mainly from partially hydrogenated oils; smaller amounts occur naturally in meat and dairy from ruminant animals.

Why BOTIA flags it

Trans fats raise LDL cholesterol and lower HDL, increasing cardiovascular risk — while women were historically underrepresented in coronary heart-disease research. They have also been associated with systemic inflammation, endometriosis and ovulatory infertility, although those are associations, not proof of causation. Industrial trans fats come mainly from partially hydrogenated oils; ruminant trans fats occur naturally in meat and dairy, usually in much smaller amounts.

BOTIA CAN

  • Detect partially hydrogenated oils or an explicit trans-fat declaration when they appear on the label.
  • Explain the cardiovascular evidence and the associations reported in women with systemic inflammation, endometriosis and ovulatory infertility.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Determine the exact amount of trans fat from an ingredient list when the quantity is not declared.
  • Prove that trans-fat intake caused endometriosis, infertility or inflammation in a particular person.
