Total sugars

What it is

Total sugars covers every sugar in the product, wherever it came from. Those that were added and those already present in the ingredients. That is why plain yoghurt with no added sugar declares sugars: the lactose in milk counts. And so does freshly squeezed juice with nothing else: the fructose in the fruit counts the same. That does not make them equivalent to a soft drink with the same figure. But the label alone does not tell them apart. Separating them requires other fields. Added sugars are declared in the United States, Brazil, India and some other markets. Free sugars are a WHO concept some countries use on front-of-pack warnings. Neither can be calculated from the total.

Why BOTIA flags it

BOTIA flags total sugars because it is the field that causes the most misunderstanding, and in both directions. Some people see sugars in plain yoghurt and assume something was added. Others compare two products with the same figure without knowing that in one it comes from fruit and in the other from a syrup. BOTIA shows the figure, says what it includes, and links to the ingredient list, which is where you can see where it came from.

BOTIA CAN

  • Read the declared value and keep its unit and its basis.
  • Show added or free sugars where the market declares them, as separate fields.
  • Identify added sugars and ingredients that contribute them when they are declared in the ingredient list, such as sucrose, dextrose, syrups, honey or juice concentrates.
  • Explain that the figure includes sugars naturally present in the ingredients.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Tell you what percentage of your own needs this value represents.
  • Calculate added sugars by subtracting anything from the total. It cannot be worked out: it is a field that is either declared or not.
  • Calculate free sugars from the total or from added sugars. They are three separate concepts.
  • Say how much came from each ingredient. The label declares the total.
  • Treat an undeclared value as zero.
