Sweeteners

Not sugar. But not neutral either.

Non-sugar sweeteners add sweetness without calories. They appear in products marketed as 'light', 'zero' or 'diet'. BOTIA flags them because their long-term effects are under active scientific review.

Why this matters

WHO (2023) advises against using non-sugar sweeteners for weight control. Long-term use has been associated with increased risk of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and mortality. They are authorised — but the science is not settled.

What BOTIA looks for

BOTIA detects ingredients classified as non-sugar sweeteners: aspartame (E951), acesulfame K (E950), sucralose (E955), saccharin (E954), cyclamates (E952), stevia (E960), and sugar alcohols such as sorbitol, maltitol, xylitol and erythritol. Each has a different profile — BOTIA flags their presence so you can decide.

BOTIA CAN

  • Detect non-sugar sweeteners declared on the label.
  • Flag sugar alcohols that may cause digestive effects in large quantities.
  • Signal when a 'sugar-free' product contains multiple sweeteners.

BOTIA CANNOT

  • Tell you whether sweeteners are safe for you personally.
  • Quantify your daily sweetener intake or cumulative exposure.
  • Replace medical or nutritional advice.

If you are monitoring sweetener intake, check both the ingredient list and the nutrition label.

Sources: WHO Non-Sugar Sweeteners (2023) · EFSA Sweeteners Re-evaluation
